## Chapter research goal

Give Chapter 1 a selective, source-backed opening history showing that the dream of artificial or intelligent-seeming machines is much older than modern AI, while keeping a clear boundary between cultural lineage, mechanical lineage, computational lineage, and modern AI as a named research field.

The chapter should help readers stop treating today's AI as either sudden magic or an inevitable continuation of every old automaton story. It should prepare Ch 2's feedback/control vocabulary and Ch 3's Dartmouth/modern-AI history without trying to become a full history of automata, computing, or philosophy of mind.

## Examples available

- Talos or mythic mechanical beings as the cultural opening: old dream, not technology and not AI.
- Babbage/Lovelace as programmable calculation and symbolic possibility, used briefly unless specialist sources are added.
- Turing's universal machine as a bridge toward general computation, kept high-level.
- Turing's imitation game as a reframing of machine intelligence, with the explicit caveat that imitation is not proof of understanding, reliability, or safety.
- Early chess/checkers programs as concrete task performance, with symbolic-AI detail deferred to Ch 3.
- Dartmouth as the handoff to Ch 3, not the center of Ch 1.
