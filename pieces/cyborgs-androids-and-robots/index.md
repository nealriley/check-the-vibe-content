# Cyborgs, Androids, and Robots

## The idea

A personal, speculative essay that begins with a train conversation and moves through three ways technology meets the human: robots that do work for us, androids that resemble us, and cyborgs who incorporate technology into their own bodies and sense of self. The source prose moves from automation and labour to prostheses, connected knowledge, and a playful “Cyborg Church.” The revised title puts the cyborg question first.

The distinctions can give the piece a path, but they need not become a rigid taxonomy. Its strongest question may be what changes when a tool stops feeling external and becomes part of how a person lives, perceives, or acts.

## Questions to develop

- Which scene or image from the original train story should carry the reader into the argument?
- What do *robot*, *android*, and *cyborg* each help us see, and where do the categories blur?
- How do labour and servitude enter the history and fiction of robots, and which historical claims in the scrap need checking?
- What does a prosthesis or other augmentation reveal about the boundary between a person and a tool?
- How much of the Buddhism and interconnectedness thread belongs in this piece?
