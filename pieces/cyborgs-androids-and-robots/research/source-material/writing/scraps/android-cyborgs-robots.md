# Androids, Cyborgs, and Robots

It all begins on a train, in the times of planes and cars (more on how they collide later). I am sitting in a first class carriage with a lovely riding friend, we the only two taking up spaces in a 6 car, 1st cabin. I picked up the train at its beginning in Amsterdam, headed to East Berlin to speak about automation and what it means for every person. Our friend, we will call him Jan as he seems to be such, picks up on the way in Hilversum. Hans has a few years behind him, but is quick and witted. We joke about how a lack of Wifi (which we pronounce like "Whi" of Whiffle Bat and a hard Eee) is due to Deutsche Bahn wishing to distinguish their hi-speed service from the standard service. The only other way we can think of that this is distinguished is the fact that this service is 5 hours longer, but alas. Jan is on his way to a linguistics conference, and in the middle of proofing talk submssions. I ask if he can explain it, however it is in Dutch, written by a German, thus impossible with the time (5 hours) or patience alloted. The irony was not lost on me.

That's not what this first post is about though. Cyborgs. That's the point. And Buddhism. Yes.

Let me explain. For the longest time, in fact as far back as I can remember, I've been obsessed with Robots. I associated the word with all kinds of variants of mechanical machinations. No matter, for as I get older I was blessed with a deeper understanding of the concepts that underpin such technicological marvels, and why they matter. Let's start with Robots.

My favorite pub quiz question never asked: "What medieval institution is best aligned with the original meaning of 'robot', as used to describe the automatic nature of delivering value as previously provided by humans". Turns out, the answer is "serf", a robot is in many senses a slave, bound to the lord (think feudal not biblical) who dictates how best to take the singular contributions and deliver a meaningful overall effort for the feudal land. A robot was never meant to take a share in the reward, they are meant to perform a task until their atomic structure does not allow them to perform such a task anymore, then they are replaced. This is the engine that drives economic growth without requiring all members of the society to toil, it's the origin of a distribution of forward-thinking responsiblities and rewards, away from the daily toil.

There is quite a long history of the struggle between man and robot - the industrial revolution did quite a number on outlining the potential future problems without providing any meaningful path to resolution. I wont have time in this iteration to go deeper on the Sabo and the Saboteur, however look up the origin of these words (and luddites by proxy) to see where this selection of text might be going in the future.

Enough about robots, let's talk about Automotons and the Androids. This can be a subtle difference at first, however the shared charateristics between the two seem to be a human-like (this will be further elaborated) yet technologically driven being which can perform as a human does, with areas of super-human capability.

My favorite automoton must be the Mechanical Turk - who as it turns out was complete hoax - but was billed as a mechanical automoton, forever bolted to a chess table, who could outperform the best chess masters in the world with a nearly-literal "black box" of technology lying beneath. As it turns out, there was a chess master with a plethora of knobs and dials which could make the automoton move in a meaningful way, however the mystique dazzled those who came across it, including Napolean, who was bested by the automoton. This was a technological marvel, like many robots of its time, with the status of a human, but without the convincing nature of 'being' a human being.

Here is where we differentiate these two with Androids, with a bit more modern a story. Metropolis. Beautiful film. However, I truly love the the animated retelling released in 2001. Here we see a class structure developing, which include Humans, Robots, Automotons, and a single Android, who throughout the film is convinced they are human themselves. This android, a true masterpiece of technology, comes distrubingly close to acheiving a true human appearance and demanour, but of course in a tragic and epic style falls short. Worth a watch for either to clearly outline the differences.

Finally, Cyborgs. While all the previous technolofical marvels constitue a mechanical, pre-programmed (or even developed to self-learn from pre-programming) mind, a Cyborg is a human mind/body that attach to a technological part and assume it as part of their own. A hack-comedian way of describing "kids these days" would easily describe such a concept as 'look at kids glued to their phones, its part of their bodies'. This concept is actually facinating.

As a child on the cusp of many knife edges of massive technological leaps, I was able to easily see the peril that many around me are facing. I remember a time without access to the worlds knowledge, but I also was alive long enough in a pre-teen state of human development where the nature of all human knowledge at my fingertips is still palpable. I see this manifest in people of all ages as "I read Facebook, but I do not post." The concept of sharing intimiate details is still foreign, as you were never really connected in formative years to a vast network of information, uncaring of whether you specifically were doing this specific thing on instagram at this specific time. However, I recognize the vast nature of human knowledge, and the importance of connecting with it, at least in a passive-traveller sense, in order to maintain articulate conversations with those I share no physical, historic, or hobbyist similarities with.

One such cusp, (which is not the most terrifying sadly: that would be: "One person who you know who was born today will not die in the next 500 years. May not be you, sorry tiger.") is the concept of human augmentation. We are probably nearing the last generation without cheap, effective, augmentation for the human body. This might be helping bodies regrow things in ways they might naturally do, just they needed a bit of prodding. However for this discussion, I want to talk about modification with mechanical parts.

If you are a coffee-shop buddhist (read about it, like it when caffeinated, don't travel to monasteries and give up all material belongings), then you may be well aware of the concept of a univers simultaneously apprehinding itself. You might describe your own body as a universe, and the bacteria, and other organisms that are swimming in you (neat, huh?) as the inhabitants of that universe. A bacterium may consider itself to be a separate part, but in fact it's easily possible to desribe this bacterium as a part of a whole. Well, reader, you are equally so in the universe at large. This assuming you ascribe to the aformentioned world view.

Now, cyborgs really help unlock this idea. Let's assume we are all a collection of atoms swimming around. The fact you call air "empty space" is the same as a sea creature might call water "empty". It's a medium you can easily traverse. It's all atoms. Now, what cyborgs, or augmented humans; what they really do is begin to really dig into this idea of how you can take atoms outside the ones gifted to you by DNA (or god, whatever) and turn them into a part of you. In fact, they deliver a combined you-and-word, which is much closer to the world a buddhist might see.

Suddenly, you are dealing with the world in a new way. You may interface with all kinds of parts, people, robots, androids, cyborgs...in a new way. The same way someone with a prosthesis might take on a new "arm", and that arm my simply be 3-D printed in their home, in such a way that the brain knows how to make it part of the "body". Suddenly you have a new super-human, and I cannot stress enough, not in the Man-Superman sense of this word. What if, the next stage of social evolution is this kind of behavior. First you are born, and as early as you can be healthy, you are fitted with the capability to interface into the physical world, as well as the technological world. A childs brain of any species is ready to handle such changes and growth areas, as its the fundamental nature in how beings adapt to the inputs, the outputs, and the environment they operate in. Perhaps children latch on to information because humans who do so are meant to. Perhaps they do so because this is an abboration that should have have happened to human beings this early, without a clear path to help assimilate this knowledge and access to knowledge in a meaningful way that encourages steady and stable mental and physical growth.

Dear reader, this is just a beginning. The rest remains far away.

Thus begins, a church. A Cyborg Church. On a train, in the beautiful countryside.
