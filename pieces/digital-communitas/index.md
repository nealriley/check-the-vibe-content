# Digital communitas

## The idea

A piece about the kind of shared life a digital community can build when its members can communicate almost instantly and keep a durable record of what they have said and made together. The original fragment names that combination **shared memory**. This piece can ask whether such memory helps a group develop continuity and belonging, and what happens when its record is incomplete, searchable, editable, or controlled by a platform.

“Communitas” is the new working title and a concept to explore, not a settled claim about every online group. The starting material is only a few lines; the piece's form and argument remain open.

## Questions to develop

- What can a digital group remember collectively that an in-person group usually cannot?
- Who decides which exchanges are kept, found again, forgotten, or removed?
- Does a shared archive strengthen belonging, or can it make a community feel watched and fixed in its past?
- Which actual community could make the idea concrete?
- What does “communitas” add to this idea, and is it the right word once its meaning and history have been examined?
