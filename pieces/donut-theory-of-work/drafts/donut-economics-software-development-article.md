## 4. What did not disappear

Source cues:

- [[../research#The Golden Path Behind Generated Software]] — production boundaries and operability.

Possible cost groups:

1. **Definition:** context, goals, requirements, and judgment.
2. **Production:** integration, tests, security, deployment, and recovery.
3. **Operation:** observability, support, maintenance, dependencies, and migration.
4. **Coordination:** review, handoffs, ownership, approvals, and accountability.
5. **Learning:** skill formation, apprenticeship, and retaining the ability to judge generated work.
6. **Physical inputs:** compute, energy, water, hardware, and infrastructure—only after new research.

## 8. Strongest objection

Build the objection in its strongest form:

- Agents can lower verification, migration, testing, maintenance, and support costs too.
- Cheap software broadens access and lets more people solve their own problems.
- Organisational lag and current rework may be temporary adoption costs.
- Requiring every small application to internalise enterprise-grade obligations could destroy the very abundance being celebrated.

Response questions:

- Which duties should be proportionate to consequence and lifespan?
- Which costs may legitimately disappear, and which are merely moved out of sight?
- Can the essay define a viable zone without creating another optimisation score?

`NEAL:` What evidence would change your mind about the thesis?

Possible reader questions, to refine rather than publish as a generic checklist:

2. What new software became worth making?
