# Donut Theory of Work

## The idea

An essay about what remains around a task when one visible part of it becomes cheap. AI-assisted software development is the starting case: producing code may take less effort, while deciding what to build, checking it, integrating it, maintaining it, and taking responsibility for its effects still constitute work. The new title invites a wider question about work itself: which costs genuinely fall, which move to other people or stages, and who receives the benefit?

The original project has an unresolved framing question: is “donut” an adaptation of Kate Raworth's *Doughnut Economics*, an independent metaphor for the missing middle between output and useful work, or simply a working label? Keep that choice open until the idea has been developed and the relevant framework researched. The broader title does not yet commit the piece to a formal economic model.

## Questions to develop

- What does the donut depict: a hole, a ring, an inner floor and outer limit, or something else?
- Which parts of work become cheaper with AI, and which become more visible or more demanding?
- Where do saved time, money, and effort go: to workers, customers, employers, or shared infrastructure?
- What obligations remain even when a useful output can be made quickly?
- Does the software case support a general theory of work, or should it remain the piece's main scope?
