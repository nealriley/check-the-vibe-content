# The Productivity Lag Is the Work

## The seed
Powerful technologies do not become economy-wide productivity by existing. The gap is filled by complementary work: new software, redesigned processes, training, management changes, measurement, and the slow retirement of old assumptions.

AI's apparent productivity paradox may therefore be less a verdict on capability than evidence that adoption is an organisational transformation problem. Task-level speedups, firm-level gains, labour-market changes, and aggregate productivity are different outcomes on different clocks.

## Recovered direction

The recovered writing pack contains editorial guardrails, source notes, post structures, social copy, and a short draft. Its key discipline is to distinguish AI capability, adoption, task gains, and economy-wide productivity without treating the PC analogy as proof that an AI boom is inevitable.

## Angles

- The missing middle between an AI demo and a changed organisation.
- Why workflow redesign, not model access, is often the binding constraint.
- Complementary investment as the unglamorous source of future productivity.
- The risk of using historical lag as an unfalsifiable excuse for weak results.
- A four-level scorecard: capability, task, firm, and economy.
