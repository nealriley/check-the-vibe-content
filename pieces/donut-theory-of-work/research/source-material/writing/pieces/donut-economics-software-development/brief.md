## Reader problem

Public discussion often collapses several different propositions into “AI makes software cheaper.” A reader needs a way to distinguish:

- cheaper generation from cheaper operation;
- task speed from workflow, firm, or economy-wide productivity;
- new capability from durable value;
- a gain in output from a gain received by a worker or customer;
- a visible price reduction from costs displaced into review, maintenance, risk, shared infrastructure, or environmental inputs.

## Reader promise

Give the reader a practical economic map for asking what becomes abundant, what remains scarce, which costs are hidden or displaced, and who captures the gain when software implementation becomes cheaper.

## Candidate claims

1. **Cheap code is not free software.** As implementation cost falls, definition, verification, integration, security, operation, maintenance, and accountability form more of the total cost.
2. **Lower implementation cost changes what is worth building.** Software can serve one household, revive an obsolete teaching tool, or disappear when a temporary problem ends without being a failed product.
3. **Productivity is a distribution question.** Faster development has different consequences when its surplus becomes customer value, developer time, vendor margin, reduced staffing, or a higher quota.
4. **The unit of value is moving from access and effort toward useful work.** Usage and outcome measures can improve alignment, but only when quality, attribution, and downstream responsibility are auditable.
5. **An abundant software economy still depends on replenishment.** Maintainers, community knowledge, junior skill formation, organisational learning, and physical compute infrastructure do not reproduce themselves merely because code generation accelerates.

## Necessary counterarguments

- Coding agents have enabled useful work that was previously uneconomic or impossible.
- Verification and maintenance may also become cheaper; current cost allocations are not permanent laws.
- Task-level gains can be real even when firm or aggregate gains lag.
- Outcome pricing can align incentives when an outcome is narrow, quality-adjusted, and independently auditable.
- Personal software can broaden access and agency, while also creating privacy, security, dependency, and maintenance debt.
- Knowledge-worker survey findings cannot be presented as developer-specific findings.

## Boundaries

- Keep the subjective meaning of work and the “almost a non-event” story subordinate; that belongs to the aliveness piece.
- Keep the solo-builder-as-team organisational thesis subordinate; that belongs to the entrepreneur/squad piece.
- Keep generated style, “thusness,” and software monoculture subordinate; that belongs to creativity and conformity.
- Do not turn this into a general AI-pricing explainer. Pricing is evidence for value allocation, not the whole subject.
