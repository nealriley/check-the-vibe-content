## Evidence policy

The current repository contains four different kinds of material. Keep them distinct in drafting:

1. **Original notes and testimony** — Neal's words or firsthand narrative material.
2. **Existing drafts** — reusable language and argument, but not independent evidence.
3. **External-source notes preserved locally** — summaries, extracts, and fact banks that retain links or provenance; consult the original before publication where indicated.
4. **Unverified leads** — ideas or references that require new source work before they can support a claim.

## Original notes and testimony

### [[../../ideas/navigating-by-aliveness/interview-notes|Navigating by Aliveness interview notes]]

What it contributes:

- A working Mac audio application that Neal could not otherwise have implemented.
- The result was useful and made him happy, yet the achievement became “almost a non-event.”
- His role felt like “a consumer picking their menu items”; his contribution was “only the context and the stated goal”; he called it “a teamless group project.”
- Both older and newer practices depended on community code, preventing a simplistic independent-author versus borrowed-code distinction.

Use here:

- Possible opening evidence that implementation cost and felt or organisational value do not move in lockstep.
- Evidence that community inputs remain present even when project-specific implementation is generated.

Boundary:

- The phenomenology of aliveness, reciprocity, and meaningful participation belongs in the aliveness piece.
- The interview has not answered where saved time went; do not invent the answer.

Source type: intake idea front page summarising Adam Waxman's essay.

Supported direction:

- Lower implementation cost makes tiny-audience and short-lived software economically rational.
- Building may function as craft or care rather than startup formation.

### [[../../ideas/coding-agents-software-preservation/context|Working Context: Old and New Apps via Modern Coding Agents]]

`REVIEW:` Consult Tao's original post and linked making-of pages before publication.

### [[../../ideas/ai-productivity-lag/source-notes|AI Productivity Lag: Source Notes and Claim Checks]]

Source type: recovered source notes based on a Stanford SIEPR policy brief and its cited productivity literature.

Supported direction:

- Capability, adoption, productive use, firm productivity, and aggregate productivity are different measures.
- Complementary investment can delay measured gains.
- The productivity J-curve is an explanation for a possible lag, not proof of a future payoff.

Caveats already preserved:

- AI need not follow the PC timetable.
- Verification, errors, security, homogenisation, and displaced learning can offset task gains.

### [[../../ideas/cio-ai-services-article/author-profile-and-related-coverage|Grant Gross author profile and related coverage]]

Source type: locally preserved coverage map and article summaries.

Supported direction:
