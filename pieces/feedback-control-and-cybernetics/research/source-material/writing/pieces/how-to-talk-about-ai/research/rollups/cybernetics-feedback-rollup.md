## Area purpose

Support Chapter 2 by introducing cybernetics and feedback/control vocabulary as a durable way to understand systems that sense, compare, communicate, and adjust.

## Core findings so far

- Wiener’s 1948 _Cybernetics_ is a canonical historical anchor for “control and communication” in animals and machines.
- Feedback can be explained as information about system behavior used by a controller to adjust future behavior toward a goal or standard.
- Cybernetics is useful background for AI, control systems, feedback loops, agents, and evaluation loops, but it should not be collapsed into modern AI.
- Recommended example sequence: steering or thermostat, reaching for an object, a modern digital feedback example, then a teaser for later learning/agent loops.
- The current boundary rule is “the overlap is a bridge, not an identity”: feedback loops can appear in AI systems, but a feedback loop is not automatically AI.

## Approved language / draftable explanation

Cybernetics gave mid-20th-century researchers a vocabulary for steering systems: information comes in, the system compares what is happening with what should happen, and some controller adjusts behavior. That idea matters for AI literacy because many AI systems are surrounded by loops of feedback, evaluation, correction, and control. But cybernetics is a historical neighbor and influence, not simply an old name for today’s AI.
