# From Scraping Defence to Participation Economics

## The idea

What would it take to turn unwanted machine traffic into an explicit relationship between a site and an agent? The writer's existing draft starts with AI scraping and argues for identifying machine participants, setting permissions, offering useful machine-readable access, and sometimes charging for it. [Emma Roth's 17 October 2026 report for *The Verge*](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1013195/ai-agents-blocked-web) adds a sharper problem: personal agents are also being blocked when they try to browse, book, buy, or use accounts for a person.

The piece should ask what a site and a user need to know before an agent can participate: **who it represents, what it may read or do, what terms apply, and who is accountable**. Crawling public content and acting on a customer's account are different activities. The draft's access models may help with both, but a paid crawler feed alone does not authorize a purchase or solve account security.
