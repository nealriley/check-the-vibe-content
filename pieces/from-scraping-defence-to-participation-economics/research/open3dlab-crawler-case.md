# Open3DLab: the costs and consent behind machine access

Added 27 October 2026. Source: Salaryman, [“Roblox downloaded 145 GB of models from our sites (including adult content) without asking,” Open3DLab](https://open3dlab.com/news/roblox-downloaded-145-gb-of-models-from-our-sites/), published and last updated 26 October 2026.

## Reported activity and limits

Open3DLab reports that a crawler downloaded 1,027 files from 712 projects across its three sites on 15–16 October: 145.5 GB, including adult assets and 423 projects marked for non-commercial use. It says the crawler retrieved but disregarded robots.txt, breached its scraping terms, and saturated free download connections, disrupting ordinary users. The operators say they blocked further access and received no response to enquiries by publication.

The crawler identified itself with a Roblox work address in its user agent but ran from AWS. The operators explicitly acknowledge that this identity is self-declared. They infer a connection to model research but cannot establish training or evaluation use. The post is a firsthand operator account; its underlying logs have not been independently audited here. It establishes no legal finding.

## What this adds to the essay

The draft should give the host's costs and creators' choices weight alongside the opportunity to serve a machine audience. A valuable dataset does not automatically produce a willing trading relationship. Participation needs terms that both sides can accept, including the possibility of refusal.

This case also complicates the phrase *content owner*. A platform may host a file without holding every permission needed to license it for another use. A paid access arrangement would need to account for the creator's rights and any third-party material, as well as the cost of delivery.

## Questions to carry forward

- What would verify a crawler's operator beyond an asserted identity?
- Who can authorize which uses: the host, the uploader, or another rights holder?
- How would an agreed service protect ordinary users' access and allocate infrastructure costs?
- Would offered payment change the host's or creators' decision, and what happens when they decline?
- Can an agreement specify provenance, permitted uses, retention, and deletion obligations in a way participants can check?
