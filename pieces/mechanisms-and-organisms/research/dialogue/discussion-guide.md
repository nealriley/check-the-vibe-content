# Discussion guide

Topics for a working conversation between the writer and Claude about [Seeing Through the Net](../source-material/seeing-through-the-net-organism-earth.md), before writing the brief. The conversation is recorded in [the dialogue log](dialogue-log.md).

**The lens (set by the writer, 1 November 2026):** read the lecture against modern times. That means how businesses run now, how far technology has come since 1969, and specifically how AI has been introduced into work. No single theme leads. Mechanism versus organism is one topic among many, not the spine.

## Round 3: the key ideas, in dialogue (from 1 November 2026)

The writer rejected the Round 2 question format as well. Claude now pulls the key ideas out of the talk and takes the writer through them one by one. For each idea, Claude brings Watts's point and its own thinking about where it lands today, as material to react to. It is a conversation to spark new ideas, not an interview. The Round 2 questions and audits are recorded in [the dialogue log](dialogue-log.md#change-of-approach).

Each idea is covered in three parts: **Watts** (the idea in a line or two), **Where it lands now** (Claude's thinking, offered as possibilities rather than conclusions), and **Thread** (a direction to pull on, which the writer can take or leave).

1. **Who guards the guards.** Control breeds more control until "we're standing in our own way". (talk map beats 1–3)
2. **What would you do if you were God?** The dream experiment: total control ends in choosing to be surprised. (beats 6–8)
3. **Made or grown.** "How was I made?" against "How was I grown?", the mechanism against the organism, and the gallbladder specialist. (beats 10, 16)
4. **The net.** We "bitted the cosmos" with grids, measurement and words. A thing is a "think". Spotlight against floodlight. (beats 11–12, 14–15)
5. **Prickles and goo.** Two temperaments, precise and vague, and nature is both depending on the magnification. (beat 13)
6. **The information explosion.** Who has read all the catalogues? Parkinson's Law means computers keep you ahead "only for a short time". (beats 17–18)
7. **Our strongest suit.** Machines can't read a handwritten "a" and lack "field organisation". Are we neglecting what the brain does best? And which kinds of intelligence do we reward? (beats 19, 25)
8. **The teeth can't bite themselves.** Self-reference, Gödel, and why total self-knowledge would leave no surprise. (beats 20–21)
9. **Answers first, checking after.** The brain hands you the answer, and the bit-by-bit checking comes later, often too late. (beats 22–23)
10. **Right foot, left foot.** Not anti-technology: alternate the analytic with respect for organic organisation, or become the dinosaur with two brains. (beats 24, 26)
11. **Trust, and the menu and the dinner.** Distrust projects control into clocks and institutions. We confuse the symbol with the reality. (beats 27–29)

After the ideas: debate the format (the five candidate shapes recorded in the log's "Change of approach" section), where Watts sits in the piece, and the title.

## How businesses run

### A. The paperwork problem, 57 years on

*Watts:* the small business drowning in legal hassles, the hospital with no time for medicine, the university smothered by the registrar. "Obstructed by his own cautiousness." (beat 3)

- Did half a century of computing reduce this, or multiply it?
- Is AI being brought in to remove the paperwork, or to produce it faster?
- Where is it worse now: compliance, reporting, documentation, meetings about meetings?

**Opening position:** AI is mostly being introduced to do the paperwork faster, not to ask whether it should exist. Watts's opening complaint is the most recognisable sentence in the talk for a 2026 manager, which makes it a strong way in.

### B. Who guards the guards?

*Watts:* the problem of control. Control systems built from distrust are "really ourselves, disguised". (beats 1, 27)

- How do modern companies control work: approvals, audits, OKRs, tracking software, access policies?
- When AI arrives, do companies add controls for the AI, for the people using it, or both?
- Who in a business now guards the guards? Who checks the dashboards and the evaluators?

**Opening position:** AI adoption has mostly added control layers (usage policies, approved-tool lists, review steps) before it removed any. The organisation's first response to a new capability is to govern it, which is Watts's point in action.

### C. The specialist and the gallbladder

*Watts:* the specialist who understands one organ and can't foresee the effects elsewhere, because a body is not a car with replaceable spark plugs. (beat 16)

- Are modern organisations built as replaceable parts: roles, teams, microservices, vendors, outsourced functions?
- When AI replaces one task or role, who sees the effects elsewhere in the company?
- Is "drop an agent in where a person was" the spark-plug view of work?

**Opening position:** this is where the mechanism/organism idea matters to business. Companies plan AI roll-outs as part replacements on an org chart, but they behave like organisms. The unintended effects show up in culture, training, junior pipelines and trust.

### D. The menu and the dinner

*Watts:* the clock as authority, and confusing the symbol with the reality: "the money with the wealth, and the menu with the dinner… we're starving on eating menus." (beats 28–29)

- What are the business menus now: KPIs, dashboards, engagement scores, utilisation, lines of code, AI usage stats?
- Is "AI adoption" itself being measured as a menu, with tokens used and seats licensed standing in for value?
- Where have you seen the measurement replace the thing?

**Opening position:** the AI era has a ready-made menu problem. Companies report adoption rates and token spend because those are countable, while the dinner (better work, better decisions) is hard to see. This links to [Trading Seats for Tokens](../../../trading-seats-for-tokens/index.md).

## How technology has come along

### E. The information explosion and Parkinson's law

*Watts:* "Who has read all the catalogs?" Computers keep you ahead "only for a short time". The drummers out-complicate each other until a gadget has to keep count. (beats 17–18)

- Was he right? Every efficiency gain since 1969 seems to have produced more to keep track of.
- Has AI broken the cycle by reading everything, or sped it up by writing everything?
- What does this look like inside a business: inboxes, Slack, reports summarised by AI and then summarised again?

**Opening position:** this is the beat where he was most clearly right. AI is the drummers' contest at machine speed, with machines writing documents for other machines to summarise.

### F. What the machine can now do

*Watts:* computers can't read a handwritten "a"; they lack "field organization"; they're all dots, like a newspaper photograph. The brain is our "strongest suit". (beats 19–20)

- He was plainly wrong about the limit. Does that weaken the rest of the talk, or sharpen it?
- If machines now have pattern recognition, what is the human's strongest suit at work?
- Do businesses understand what has changed, or do they still treat AI as a faster calculator?

**Opening position:** being wrong about the "a" is useful. It shows exactly what changed technically, and it lets the piece ask whether the business culture around the technology changed as much. It probably hasn't.

### G. Made or grown

*Watts:* "How was I made?" against "How was I grown?" A mechanism is assembled; an organism grows. (beats 10, 16)

- Today's models are trained rather than programmed. Does that matter to anyone outside the labs?
- Businesses buy AI as a product with versions and specs. Is that a mismatch with how it behaves?
- Are companies themselves mechanisms or organisms, and does AI push them one way?

**Opening position:** one topic among many. It's most useful as a contrast: the technology became more grown, while the way businesses deploy it stayed assembled.

## How AI has been introduced

### H. Answers first, checking after

*Watts:* the brain hands you the answer, then you check it bit by bit, because only that counts socially, and the checking often comes "much too late". (beats 22–23)

- With AI, the machine gives the fast answer and the person checks it. What has that done to work?
- Is "human in the loop" a real role, or a sign-off ritual?
- What happens to judgement when most of the job is reviewing?

**Opening position:** the most recognisable change for knowledge workers. It's also the most personal, and it links to [The Loss of Aliveness](../../../the-loss-of-aliveness/index.md).

### I. Right foot, left foot

*Watts:* not anti-technology. The technological enterprise put the right foot forward; now bring up the left, "a new respect for the organic kind of organization". Engineers must tell the politicians the limits. (beat 24)

- What would the left foot look like in a company adopting AI?
- Who plays the engineers' role now: telling leadership what AI can't do?
- Is the AI roll-out all right foot: speed, efficiency, headcount?

**Opening position:** this gives the piece a constructive stance rather than a lament. The question is what a balanced introduction of AI looks like, not whether to introduce it.

### J. What do you actually want?

*Watts:* if you're God, what do you want? Most people have never asked. The dream thought experiment ends in boredom and choosing to lose control. (beats 6–8, 21)

- Do businesses know what they want from AI beyond "efficiency" and "fewer people"?
- What happens when the friction goes: when everything is on demand?
- Is "we can't say what we want" the real bottleneck, rather than what the technology can do?

**Opening position:** most AI strategies are "how" documents with no "what for". Watts's twenty-page essay on your idea of heaven is a useful exercise for a leadership team.

### K. Kinds of intelligence

*Watts:* Lynn White: education values verbal, mnemonic and computational intelligence, and neglects the kinaesthetic and social. Knowing how without knowing why. (beat 25)

- The three valued intelligences are what AI now does well. Do hiring and promotion follow?
- Does AI make social and tacit skills more valuable at work, or just harder to measure?

**Opening position:** a crowded topic in AI writing. Worth a pass through the business lens (hiring, juniors, career ladders) rather than the education lens.

## Afterwards: form and voice

Once the topics are explored: how visible Watts is, tone, structure (then, then now), how to handle the dated parts of the talk (broad East/West generalisations and the "pale-face" anecdote), and the title. This becomes the brief's approach section.
