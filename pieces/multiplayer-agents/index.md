# Multiplayer Agents

## The idea

Most personal agents are organised around one person: their context, preferences, permissions, and tasks. What changes when an agent works with a **group of people** and carries shared context forward for them? A family might ask it to coordinate life at home; friends might use it to plan and remember decisions together; a business team might rely on it for project continuity and follow-through.

The interesting change is social as well as technical. A shared agent has to know what belongs to the group, what remains private to each person, whose instructions it may act on, and how to handle disagreement. It can hold a common record and help with common responsibilities, but the people still need clear authority over consequential decisions and actions.

This is about **one agent serving several people**, not simply several agents working for one person. The piece can use the three settings to see which problems are universal and which depend on intimacy, trust, or formal roles.

## Questions to develop

- What does a group actually want to share with an agent: memory, goals, tasks, access, or decision history?
- Who can add to, correct, or delete the shared context? What remains visible only to one member?
- Whose request takes priority when members disagree, and what should the agent do before acting for the group?
- What changes when someone joins or leaves a household, friend group, or team?
- How should the agent show who asked for an action, who approved it, and what it did?
- What would make the shared agent feel like a useful common resource rather than one person's assistant with extra accounts?
