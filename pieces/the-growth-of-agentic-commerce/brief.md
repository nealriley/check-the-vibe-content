# Working brief: The Growth of Agentic Commerce

## Reader question

What changes when a shopping assistant can remember, compare, negotiate the path to checkout, and eventually buy within limits set by a person?

## Argument to develop

The move toward agentic commerce is a sequence of delegated tasks, not a single jump from browsing to a robot shopper. A persistent cart can collect and monitor possible purchases. A common commerce protocol can help a shopping surface reach participating merchants. A separate authorization and payment layer may allow an agent to complete a transaction under the buyer's instructions. Each step changes where decisions happen and who can see or influence them.

Google's Universal Cart is the opening case. Its October 2026 U.S. rollout makes some of this infrastructure visible, but the cart is primarily an organizing and checkout aid. The essay should test the larger claim with additional services and evidence before describing a broad shift in actual consumer behavior.

## Possible shape

1. Begin with a familiar shopping problem: products scattered across tabs, stores, wish lists, and changing prices.
2. Use Universal Cart to show what an assistant can already remember and monitor, and where the shopper still decides.
3. Explain the transaction boundary: how a common protocol and merchant participation may carry an intention into checkout, and who remains the seller.
4. Explore the next boundary, explicit authority for an agent to purchase. Ask what instructions, spending limits, approvals, records, and dispute paths would make that delegation trustworthy.
5. Follow the incentives: the shopper wants a good choice; merchants want a sale and a customer relationship; the intermediary may rank products, earn advertising revenue, or own the interface.
6. End with the unresolved question of whose agent this really is and how a shopper can inspect or correct its judgment.

## Reporting and checks

- Separate announced capabilities, live product behavior, protocol specifications, and observed adoption.
- Do not describe Universal Cart itself as autonomously buying products. Check what a user must approve at checkout and which merchants participate.
- Test whether the assistant's recommendations can be explained and challenged, especially when ads, promotions, card perks, or preferred merchants affect the result.
- Investigate what information the intermediary retains across retailers, and what the merchant sees or loses in the relationship.
- Examine returns, fraud, mistaken purchases, and responsibility after a delegated transaction, using concrete examples rather than only protocol promises.
- If the essay discusses AI-generated consumer research or pricing, use the [willingness-to-pay study](research/willingness-to-pay-study.md) as a caution about synthetic shoppers. It does not measure live shopping agents or actual agent-mediated transactions.
