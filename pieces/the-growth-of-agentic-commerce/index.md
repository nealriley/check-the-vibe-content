# The Growth of Agentic Commerce

## The idea

Shopping software is beginning to do more than show products and hold a basket. It can keep track of a search across stores and sessions, watch prices and availability, suggest ways to pay, and help carry a purchase into checkout. The question for this piece is how that progression changes when a person asks an agent to act on their behalf: which choices can be delegated, what must remain visible, and who gains control of the relationship between shopper and merchant?

The immediate prompt is [TechCrunch's 15 October 2026 report on the U.S. rollout of Google's Universal Cart](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/15/googles-universal-cart-shopping-tool-launches-in-the-us/). [Google's launch note](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/shopping/universal-cart-launch/) describes the cart as a practical tool for organizing shopping and presents delegated agent actions as a related, distinct direction. This is a useful case for studying the growth of agentic commerce, not proof that the cart itself makes autonomous purchases or that shoppers have adopted such behavior at scale.
