# What Would an AI Have to Express?

## The seed
Debates about AI art often stop at output: is the image novel, moving, or indistinguishable from human work? Jo Marchant's reporting and conversation with computational-creativity researcher Simon Colton point toward a harder criterion: creativity may involve an agent choosing what to make and drawing on its own encounters, purposes, or experience as material.

That changes the question from "Can an AI produce art?" to "What, if anything, is the system trying to express—and where did that expressive project come from?"

## Angles

- Output novelty versus creative agency, intention, and self-directed subject matter.
- Whether art requires experience or only an audience capable of finding meaning.
- The Painting Fool and What-If Machine as alternatives to prompt-in/image-out creativity.
- Human provenance, selection, and accountability in AI-assisted work.
- Why granting systems more creative freedom also makes evaluation and responsibility harder.
- The possibility that the most interesting machine art would be alien rather than humanlike.
