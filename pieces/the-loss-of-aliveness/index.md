# The Loss of Aliveness

## The idea

A personal essay about what productivity misses when AI makes work easier. The existing material begins with a genuinely useful AI-assisted build: a Mac audio tool that Neal could not previously have made. It worked, but the moment of success barely paused the work before the next task began. That contrast opens a question about participation, satisfaction, and where saved time actually goes.

The piece should distinguish needless drudgery from difficulty that carries judgment, learning, craft, or connection. AI may expand a person's agency in one moment and flatten their experience of the work in another. The source material does not yet establish why this particular achievement felt like a near non-event, so the essay should keep that question open.

## Questions to develop

- What was present in earlier builds that made their completion worth stopping for?
- Which difficulty would the writer gladly delegate, and which would they want to stay close to?
- Who decides what happens to time saved by AI?
- How does working with an agent compare with the shared feeling of finishing something with other people?
- When has automation made someone more present in their work or relationships?
