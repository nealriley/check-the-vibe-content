# A Better Question Than "Is AI Useful?"

## The seed
Productivity is an incomplete test for technology. Oliver Burkeman's idea of "aliveness" offers another: does a tool deepen our participation in work, relationships, difficulty, and reality, or train us to experience life as a problem of optimisation?

This does not require romanticising friction. Some difficulty is needless drudgery; some is where judgment, craft, attention, and meaning live. The point is to distinguish between them before automation makes the decision for us.

## Recovered direction

The earlier Chatto pack framed AI as both an instrument and an environment. The shallow question is whether a tool is useful. The deeper one is what habits, expectations, incentives, and institutions widespread use creates. Its proposed "aliveness audit" asks what difficulty is removed, who receives the saved time, what new pressure efficiency creates, and whether agency and connection expand or contract.

## Angles

- Aliveness versus happiness, productivity, and frictionlessness.
- Whether human provenance is becoming a value category like handmade or local.
- The difference between removing drudgery and bypassing meaningful participation.
- How efficiency gains become higher output expectations rather than free time.
- A practical aliveness audit for personal and organisational AI adoption.
