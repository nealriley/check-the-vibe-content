# Brief — The Loss of Aliveness in the Pursuit of Productivity

## Core question

What does productivity fail to notice about the way a person participates in work—and where does the time or difficulty removed by AI actually go?

## Working proposition — provisional

Productivity measures the reduction of time and effort, but not the destination of what they release. Ease can enlarge a person's agency and participation, or it can turn an achievement directly into a higher expectation and another task. An “aliveness audit” would therefore ask not only what a tool removes, but who controls the saved time, which forms of contact with the work remain, and whether connection and judgment expand or contract.

This is an editorial hypothesis, not Neal's approved conclusion. [[../../ideas/navigating-by-aliveness/project-checkpoint]] records what the interview has and has not established.

## What the material currently establishes

- Claude enabled Neal to make a useful Mac audio-routing application that was previously beyond his technical reach.
- He was happy when it worked, then asked Claude to clean it up and moved to the next task. The achievement was “almost a non-event.”
- Earlier difficult builds had prompted him to stop, stare, and marvel.
- Both the older creative-coding practice and the new application relied on other people's code. Borrowing is not the dividing line.
- He contributed situated context and a stated goal, but not the project-specific implementation; he felt he was a “consumer picking their menu items” and needed to be “one layer lower.”
- Human launches and crisis work provide visible emotional reciprocity and a shared transition from building to enjoying. The agent interaction did not.
- Happiness, usefulness, authorship, proximity to making, and reciprocal participation are not interchangeable.

## Tensions the essay must preserve

- AI removed a real barrier. Accessibility and expanded agency are part of the story, not a concession buried near the end.
- Difficulty is not inherently valuable. The piece needs a concrete task Neal would gladly delegate and one whose difficulty he would protect.
- Context and a stated goal may be meaningful authorship even when implementation is delegated.
- The non-event may reflect Neal's habits, workplace expectations, interface design, or another pressure. Causation is not established.
- Simulated affect and expressive “thusness” are parked questions. The creativity piece owns the generated-writing branch.
- An argument for slower or more effortful work can become a privileged defence of friction that exhausted, disabled, or precarious workers cannot afford.

## Possible shape

1. **The record plays.** Open with the first successful AirPlay test: *Special Herbs*, the backing track to KMD's “Peach Fuzz,” and a previously impossible application working.
2. **The missing pause.** Place the genuine happiness beside cleanup and the next task; contrast this with stopping and marvelling after earlier builds.
3. **One layer lower.** Explore causal proximity without claiming that unaided making ever existed; creative coding also rested on communities and tools.
4. **A teamless group project.** Contrast the agent interaction with the faces, relief, frustration, and build-to-enjoy transition of human work. Keep reciprocity distinct from authorship.
5. **Follow the saved time.** Reconstruct where one concrete AI gain went and who chose its destination. This section cannot be drafted honestly until Neal answers.
6. **Distinguish the friction.** Pair one welcome delegation with one protected difficulty; include a positive case in which automation deepened participation.
7. **An audit, not a moral.** Offer a small set of questions for a real tool or workflow, then end in a concrete action or image rather than a universal verdict.

## Interview route

Ask one question at a time and preserve the answer verbatim before interpreting it.

1. Think of a recent working day when AI saved you a meaningful amount of time. Where did that time actually go?
2. Name one difficulty you would gladly delegate forever and one you would resist giving away. What is present in the second that is absent in the first?
3. When has automation made you more present in the work or more available to another person?
4. What decisions, feedback, mistakes, or surprises would put you “one layer lower” without requiring you to write the application unaided?
5. Who is the reader facing this choice, and what decision should the essay help them make differently?

## Editorial standard

- Do not import the generated essay's route, crossing, risk, or passage metaphors into Neal's account.
- Label external findings as contextual evidence, not proof of Neal's experience.
