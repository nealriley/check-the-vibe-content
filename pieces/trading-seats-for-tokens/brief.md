# Working brief: Trading Seats for Tokens

## Reader question

What changes in the economics of software and digital services as agents account for more consumption relative to people using them directly?

## Working angle

Follow a piece of work through the services it consumes. A human might use several applications through their interfaces, with subscriptions or seats providing access. An agent may perform parts of the same work through tools, APIs, model calls, or a browser. The work can still serve a human purpose while its visible consumer, activity pattern, and cost structure change.

The essay should investigate whether this shifts spending from access to activity, how suppliers respond when fewer people visit their interfaces, and who benefits or bears the cost when machine consumption grows. Keep the question broader than model billing: tokens are one unit within a network of services, data, infrastructure, and commercial relationships.

## Possible shape

1. Start with one workflow and trace the applications, content, data, and compute used when a person performs it directly.
2. Trace the same workflow with an agent. Identify which consumption disappears, moves elsewhere, or is added through search, retries, verification, and continuous monitoring.
3. Follow the bill. Compare seats, subscriptions, requests, credits, tokens, and completed-work charges. Ask what each unit measures and who can control the expenditure.
4. Follow the supplier's incentives. What happens to discovery, advertising, customer relationships, and pricing when an agent reaches a service through another interface?
5. Test the title against cases of substitution and expansion. Does agentic consumption reduce human access spending, increase total demand, or produce a mixture? What useful outcome does the buyer receive?

## Questions for research

- What counts as agentic consumption, and how can it be distinguished from ordinary automation or a human making a model request?
- Which services show a measured change in human use, agent activity, or spending? Over what period and for which customers?
- Does a rising agent share reflect more machine activity, less direct human use, or both?
- What happens when one human supervises many agents, or one shared agent acts for many people? What does a seat represent in either case?
- Do agent workflows reduce the cost of a completed task once retries, review, integration, and infrastructure are counted?
- Who pays for tools and content an agent consumes, and what limits or approvals make that spending understandable?
- How do suppliers price access when machine throughput differs from the human activity their plans assumed?
- Can lower costs per action increase total consumption enough to raise the final bill? Who captures the additional value?

## Evidence to collect

Look for dated vendor pricing and access terms, customer usage or billing records, and documented workflows. Compare the same useful outcome where possible. Report seats, model tokens, API calls, costs, and human time separately; they are different measurements. A vendor's new pricing plan shows an offered business model, while actual usage and spending require customer or operational evidence.
