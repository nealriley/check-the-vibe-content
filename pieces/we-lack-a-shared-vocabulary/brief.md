# Working brief: We Lack a Shared Vocabulary

## The essay

The opening essay for the series argues that a poor shared vocabulary makes AI harder to understand, assess, use, and govern. It combines the former CTV-024 language problem and CTV-028 category map with the original CTV-023 opening essay. Its immediate source is the writer's [25 October transcript](research/raw-transcript-2026-10-25.md); a [cleaned prose draft](drafts/transcript-based-draft.md) is ready for development.

## Reader question

When someone says “AI,” “model,” “agent,” or “intelligence,” what do they mean—and what changes when we ask them to be specific?

## Argument to develop

The vocabulary is not merely imprecise. It can hide meaningful differences between a model and a product, between the ability to act and the authority to act for someone, and between fluent imitation and a claim about intelligence. Those differences affect what evidence we ask for, which benefits we expect, where a system might fail, and who remains responsible.

## Shape

1. Start with the lack of shared language and its practical consequences.
2. Use a familiar brand becoming a generic label to show how distinct systems blur together.
3. Explore *agent* and *agentic*: delegated representation, independent action, permissions, and responsibility.
4. Explore *model* and the layers around it: different tasks and outputs, then services and tools built around models.
5. End on *intelligence* as a contested word. Offer a habit of asking which ability is meant rather than a final definition.

The essay should feel like an invitation into the series, not an encyclopedia of every term. Examples of protocols and tools illustrate the vocabulary problem; later pieces can explain their mechanisms.
