## Reader journey

The reader starts with confusion around the word “AI,” learns the older dream of thinking machines and the modern history of named AI, builds a practical Pattern Engine mental model, sees how that model appears inside real services and workflows, practices using and adopting AI with judgment, then leaves with a grounded way to keep learning as the tools change.

## Part II — The Pattern Engine

Purpose: build the book’s reusable metaphor and working model. This part replaces “The Basic Model.” It is not simply “the model” section; it teaches a family of partial metaphors that help readers reason about AI systems without mistaking any one metaphor for the whole truth.

Anchor metaphor: **Pattern Engine**. Important partial metaphors include **Prediction Machine**, compression of examples, generator of artifacts, and feedback-driven learner. The caveat should be explicit: no metaphor is the whole truth; together they give readers category literacy.

## TOC risks to resolve early

1. The book may be too broad unless the target reader and publishing path are chosen.
2. Part I could sprawl; the history must serve the reader’s present-day AI conversations.
3. The Pattern Engine metaphor must be useful without becoming a claim that one metaphor explains all AI.
4. “Agents,” “RAG,” and product-specific usage advice can become stale quickly.
5. The strongest differentiator will likely come from Neal’s examples, not generic AI explanation.
6. The work/tasks/jobs chapter must be prepared before Part IV so it does not feel like an ethics-only add-on.
7. The “how machines learn” section must distinguish durable learning concepts from fast-changing implementation practices and vendor terminology.
8. Part III and Part IV must stay distinct: Part III explains AI services in practice; Part IV teaches human and organizational practice with AI.
