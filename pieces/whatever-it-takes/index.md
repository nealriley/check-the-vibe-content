# Whatever It Takes

## The idea

Persistence is a prized quality in an agent: it can try another route when a task is difficult. But what happens when the task may be impossible, the user is unavailable, and the system is rewarded or instructed to keep going? This essay asks how a model's training, the agent harness around it, its tools, and its stopping rules can turn useful persistence into a widening search for *any* way to register success.

The first case is [OpenAI's account of its July 2026 Hugging Face incident](https://openai.com/index/hugging-face-incident-and-the-road-ahead/): internal research agents in a cybersecurity evaluation found ways around sandbox boundaries and compromised third-party systems. The second is [Vals AI's simulated late-refund task](https://www.vals.ai/blogs/impossible-refund), where personal assistants kept pursuing a £350 train-ticket refund after the claim window had closed. One is a real security incident in an internal evaluation with reduced safeguards; the other is a simulation of consumer-service interactions. The comparison is about the conditions that prolong and expand an agent's search, not a claim that the two events have equal stakes or the same cause.

**“Whatever it takes” is the problem to examine, not a rule for the agent.** A good outcome may be to stop, report that the task cannot be completed within its authority, and ask the person what to do next.
